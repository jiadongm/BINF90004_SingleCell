Where we are

  2. Which genes change within a cell type across dev stages?

Part 1 Annotation

Three ways to name a cluster

Manual, marker-based — find genes enriched in the cluster, look them up. Flexible, slow, needs domain knowledge.

Reference-based — map onto an annotated atlas (Azimuth, SingleR, CellTypist). Fast, reproducible, fails on cell types absent from the reference.

Soft / continuous — score every cell against every reference phenotype instead of assigning one label. Eg Φ-Space

Where are the rare cells

Possible ways to miss them:

  1. QC thresholds — endothelial and immune cells were hit hardest by percent.mt < 5
  2. Clustering — smooth muscle never formed a cluster of its own

Part 2 Composition

Do proportions change?

propeller

Your independent units are the nine donors

  1. Compute each donor’s proportion of every cell type
  2. Transform them (logit or arcsin-square-root) to Euclidean space
  3. Fit a linear model per cell type, borrowing information across cell types (empirical Bayes machinery, same as limma)
  4. t-test for two groups; ANOVA F-test for more than two

Phipson, Sim, Porrello, Hewitt, Powell & Oshlack (2022), propeller: testing for differences in cell type proportions in single cell data. Bioinformatics 38(20):4720–4726.

Step 3 is the part worth a sentence: with three donors a group you cannot estimate a variance per cell type, so propeller pools that information across cell types. Same trick as limma in bulk RNA-seq, same reason - not enough replicates to go it alone.

Step 1 is the whole point. Everything else is bookkeeping on nine numbers.

Worth saying: this is the Phipson lab’s method, and the practicals are adapted from their workshop. CHECK the author list before claiming the dataset authors are on it.

Compositional bias

Cardiomyocyte nuclei fell from ~74% to ~24%. Every other proportion has to rise, whether or not anything happened to those cells.


propeller does not control for this. It stabilises the variance and fits one linear model per cell type.

Part 3 Differential expression

The question

Within cardiomyocytes: which genes differ between adult and foetal hearts?


Most commonly used: Seurat::FindMarkers(). On this data it returns ~2,000 significant genes.

That number is inflated.

The problem of pseudoreplication

FindMarkers treats every nucleus as an independent replicate.

They are not. Nuclei from one donor share:

  • that donor’s genome
  • their age, sex, medication, disease history
  • one tissue dissociation, one library prep, one sequencing run


Effective sample size is 3 donors per group, not thousandsd of nuclei.

The agent knew this

One of the ‘caveats’ reported:

“Wilcoxon on individual cells treats cells as replicates and inflates significance — for anything you’d report, redo this as donor-level pseudobulk.”

Its headline, same report: “Adult vs. foetal cardiomyocytes: 5,967 significant genes”. But it didn’t do anything.

What can we do: pseudobulking

Sum counts within each donor × cell type. One profile per donor.


Then use methods built for a handful of replicates — which is what bulk RNA-seq methods are. edgeR, limma-voom, DESeq2.


Nine columns instead of seven thousand. That was always the real sample size.

We will see results in practical.

Squair et al. (2021), Confronting false discoveries in single-cell differential expression. Nature Communications 12:5692.

Part 4 Where the field is going

Beyond discrete labels

Trajectory / pseudotime — order cells along a continuous process rather than binning them. Slingshot, Monocle3, DPT, RNA velocity; our own PhiSpace.

Cell–cell interaction — infer signalling between cell types from ligand–receptor expression. CellPhoneDB, CellChat.

Cell-specific networks — a co-expression network per cell rather than per cluster. Our own NeighbourNet.

Spatial transcriptomics

scRNA-seq tells you which cells are doing what.

Spatial tells you where they are doing it.

  • Sequencing-based: Visium, Visium HD, Slide-seq
  • Imaging-based: Xenium, CosMx, MERFISH

Tumour microenvironment, tissue architecture, niches — questions dissociation destroys.

Foundation models

scGPT, Geneformer, scFoundation, UCE — transformers pre-trained on tens of millions of cells.

Promise: transfer to new tissues, few-shot annotation, in-silico perturbation.

Open question: do they beat well-tuned simple baselines? For several tasks the honest answer is still “not clearly”.

This might be a training data issue.
